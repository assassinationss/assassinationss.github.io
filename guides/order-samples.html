How to Order Samples from China (Without Getting Played)

By Sol Research Desk · free guide · toolkit repo

The middle step everyone rushes — and where the quality story of your whole order gets written. Maintained by Sol Research Desk. Last updated: September 2026.

The sample stage is not about the product. It's about watching how the supplier behaves when the stakes are small — because that behavior, scaled up, is exactly what your production order will feel like.

Here's the full playbook: the economics, the three sample types, the traps, and the email sequence that converts a sample into a written specification.

The economics: who pays for what

Market-standard terms, and the deviations that mean something:

CostWho normally paysWhat a deviation tells you
Stock sampleBuyer, often free or $5–30"Free sample!" costs them little — friendliness, not generosity. Fine.
Customized sampleBuyer, $30–200+ depending on toolingRefusing to quote it at all = they're a trader with no line access
Courier (DHL/FedEx)Buyer, to supplier's freight account or your own"We'll pay shipping" is rare and fine; insisting on their courier account with no tracking visibility is not
Sample fee offsetCommonly credited against the bulk orderWorth asking, in writing, before — not after

The rule that matters: pay sample costs through traceable channels (PayPal, Alibaba Trade Assurance, card), never a wire to a personal account "just for the small amount." Small-money payment requests are a classic trust warm-up for the big deposit later. If the sample payment pattern looks like a rehearsal for a bad wire, treat it as one.

The three sample types (and what each is actually for)

1. Stock sample — what they already make. Purpose: baseline quality and communication speed. Weakness: proves nothing about customization or your spec.

2. Customized sample — made to your spec (logo, material, dimensions). Purpose: tests engineering comprehension. The gap between your drawing and their execution is the single most honest data point you'll get before production.

3. Golden sample — the sealed, signed reference both sides agree defines "acceptable." Purpose: the referee. When the bulk order arrives off-spec, the golden sample converts "looks different to me" into an objective dispute with evidence.

The sequence is not optional: stock → custom → golden. Skipping to custom with an unverified supplier wastes the fee; skipping the golden sample before a bulk wire is how entire containers become arguments.

The traps

The photo sample. "We'll send photos instead" — a real sample costs a real courier fee; a photo costs nothing. Photos are fine in addition, never instead. Reply: "Photos appreciated — please also send the physical sample; we'll arrange the pickup on our DHL account." (Arranging your own pickup removes their last excuse.)

The bait sample. A perfect sample from line A, then production runs on line C. Counter: the golden sample + a first-article inspection (check 5–10 units of the bulk run against the sealed sample before the rest ships). State it in the PO: "Bulk must conform to the sealed golden sample; first-article photos required before final balance payment."

The silence after payment. Sample fee paid, then the update emails stretch from days to a week. You just paid for their responsiveness test — and they failed it while the money was small. Cheap lesson; buy it now, don't buy it at deposit size.

The email sequence (4 messages, each with a job)

Message 1 — the request. Spec, quantity, deadline, and the magic question: "Is this from your own production line, or a partner factory's?" (The reply's hedging tells you which you're talking to — see what suppliers say vs. what they mean.)

Message 2 — the terms. Sample fee, courier, offset terms, payment method. Get all three in one written reply, not three. A supplier who can't answer a three-part question in one email is showing you their production-update style.

Message 3 — the spec sheet. Send your tolerances and acceptance criteria before they make the sample. This turns the sample from "their best effort" into "a compliance test." It also, incidentally, tests whether they read.

Message 4 — the golden sample letter. On receiving a good sample: "We confirm this sample as the golden standard for the bulk order. Please sign and return, keep one sealed copy on file." Simple, dated, countersigned — the cheapest insurance in sourcing.
